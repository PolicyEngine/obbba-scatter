Working paper

Household and geographic variation in federal tax and transfer policy: analysis of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act

The paper decomposes the One Big Beautiful Bill Act into 21 modeled components against a TCJA-expiration counterfactual: household resources rise $546 billion in 2026, 83% of households gain and 4% lose, the bottom equivalized-income decile loses on average once program exits count, and district averages range from roughly $150 (NY-15) to $5,800 (NH-02). Presented at the IARIW 39th General Conference (Brussels, August 28, 2026); the embedded snapshot is the revised working paper of August 30, 2026, which changes no headline number from the conference draft (its Appendix B lists the revisions). The companion pipeline repository opens at publication and is shared on request.
